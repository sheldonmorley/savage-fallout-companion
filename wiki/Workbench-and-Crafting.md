# Workbench and Crafting

Crafting, breaking down Junk and installing mods all happen in one window, the **Workbench**. No other modules are needed.

## Opening it

- **At a bench**: the GM drags a station from the **Fallout Workbench Stations** compendium onto the map: Weapons Workbench, Armor Workbench, Power Armor Station, Chemistry Station, Cooking Station or Robot Workbench. A player double-clicks it, and the Workbench opens at that bench for their character, with the bench's art across the top. Each bench shows only its own recipes.
- **Anywhere else**: the hammer button in the Token Controls, the Workbench entry in a character sheet's menu, or the **Open Workbench** macro opens the **C.A.M.P.** Away from a bench you can cook and break down Junk. With a Deluxe Toolkit, you can also install and remove mods there, at −2.

The Workbench uses the selected token's character, or your assigned character, or asks.

## Which recipes you know

- **No Edge needed**: everyone knows these.
- **Edge recipes**: taking a crafting Edge (Gun Nut, Blacksmith, Armorer, Chemist, Science!, Robotics Expert and the Improved versions) teaches all of that Edge's recipes.
- **Rare recipes** must be found or taught: lost chem formulas, rare feasts, Ballistic Weave, and every Science! Masterwork recipe (28 in all).
- A recipe that needs an Edge you lack can't be crafted, even if you've learned it.

### Found recipes

Every rare recipe is also an item, in **Fallout Crafting Materials > Rare Recipes**: *Formula:* for chems, *Recipe:* for dishes, *Schematic:* for gear and mods. The GM hands them out as loot or rewards. Use one from your inventory (or click **Learn** on the Pip-Boy, under ITEMS > MISC) and your character learns it; a whispered note says where to craft it. One copy teaches one character and is used up. If you already know it, or lack an Edge it needs, nothing is used up: keep it, sell it, or pass it on.

### Teaching (GM)

The GM can also teach a rare recipe directly. Open the Workbench for the character at the recipe's bench; as GM you see every recipe (with a *GM view* badge). Pick a rare recipe and click **Teach this recipe** on its card, or **Forget this recipe** to take it back. The button stays locked until the character has the recipe's Edge.

## Crafting

1. Pick a recipe from the search box: type part of a name, or open the list. Each recipe has a dot: **green** ready, **red** missing materials, **grey** needs an Edge, **gold** a rare recipe you haven't learned. *Only what I can craft* shows just the green ones.
2. The recipe card shows the item and what it does, its ingredients (what you have against what it needs), the roll and its modifiers, the time and any Edge. When a recipe offers a choice of ingredient, click the one to use.
3. Click **Craft**. SWADE's normal roll window opens with the recipe's modifiers already listed.

| Result | What happens |
|---|---|
| Success | The materials come off the sheet and the item goes on. |
| Raise | Also halves the time (a quarter with the governing Edge) and makes a second serving of a Camp Meal. |
| Failure | A salvage roll at −2 saves half the materials; otherwise they're lost. |
| Critical Failure | The materials are lost and the Crafting Mishap table is rolled. |

The roll includes Complexity (Simple +1, Basic 0, Advanced −1, Complex −2, Masterwork −4) and +2 for the governing Edge. Mr. Fix It's +2 is already on Repair and doesn't stack with it. Wounds and Fatigue apply.

## Breaking down Junk

Click **Break Down Junk** in the Workbench, or run the **Break Down Junk** macro. There's no roll.

- Choose any amount of Junk, plus unwanted weapons, armor and gear, which count as Junk worth half their price. Worn and wielded items aren't offered; unequip them first.
- It works at any workbench, or anywhere with a Toolkit, Multi-Tool or Deluxe Toolkit.
- Every 10 caps of Junk gives **5 Common and 1 Uncommon** Resources; a last part-lot gives its share, rounded down. **Scrapper** (+1 Uncommon per 10 caps, half the time) and **Improved Scrapper** (+1 Rare per 20 caps) are applied for you.

## Installing and removing mods

The Weapons Workbench, Armor Workbench and Power Armor Station have a **Craft / Mods** switch.

- Pick the weapon, armor or power armor from the row along the top. Its slots appear below: Sights, Receiver, Barrel, Capacitor, Grip/Stock, Magazine, Muzzle for weapons; Material, Lining, Weave, Upgrade for armor. Power armor shows its Mod slots as a meter.
- **Drag a mod onto its slot** (the right slot lights up), or click the mod and then the slot. Mods that can't go on this item are greyed out with the reason.
- Installing rolls Repair (Science for high-tech mods). Success uses up the mod and rebuilds the item with it. Failure changes nothing.
- To take a mod out intact, drag it back to your mods, or click its ×. Same roll; the mod goes back to your inventory.
- The sheet updates Damage, AP, Range, RoF, Shots, Min Str, Armor, Toughness, qualities, weight and value, and renames the item from its mods, Fallout style (a *Calibrated 10mm Pistol* with a Large Magazine becomes a *Large Calibrated 10mm Pistol*). Rename it yourself and your name sticks.

## Robots

The **Robot Workbench**'s Mods view installs robot mods on a robot you own (or on yourself, if you're a Mister Handy). Any hero can install one; crafting them needs Robotics Expert, which also adds +2. It takes an hour and a Repair roll (anywhere with a Deluxe Toolkit, at −2).

- Mod Slots: Eyebot 1, Protectron 2, Sentry Bot 4, other models 3.
- Armor Plating and skill and Pace bonuses go on automatically; other mods are listed on the robot and played as written.
- **New Automatron** (at the Robot Workbench, or for the GM) creates a bare chassis to build up with Automatron parts.
